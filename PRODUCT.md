# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary reader is someone deciding where to get cheese blintzes in New York City. They need to compare credible options by neighborhood, price, preparation, and evidence strength without reading a long methodology explanation.

## Product Purpose

JackMaguire.org publishes direct, practical writing about NYC food and other subjects Jack follows. This article should help a reader choose a cheese-blintz destination quickly and confidently. Success means the recommendations are current, useful, easy to scan, and honest about evidence limits.

## Positioning

The guide combines dish-specific research, current menu verification, and a strict publication-exclusion screen. It prioritizes useful restaurant decisions over fame, review volume, or generic listicle consensus.

## Operating Context

Readers may arrive from search, the site article feed, RSS, or a shared link. They are likely reading on a phone while planning a meal. The article should put the best choices, exact orders, neighborhoods, prices, and meaningful drawbacks near the top.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- The article is a reported guide, not a first-person restaurant diary.
- It must not claim that Jack visited or tasted any listed restaurant.
- It should not include a methodology section or personal rationale.
- Every included restaurant must have current menu and status evidence.
- The frozen publication-exclusion pack is Michelin Guide NYC, Eater NY, and The Infatuation NYC.
- An identity-confirmed editorial mention in any frozen-pack publisher excludes the restaurant.
- The final headline and item count must follow the defensible evidence. The article must not pad the list to reach 25.
- The article must appear in the main blog feed, RSS, sitemap, and relevant homepage writing feed.
- The page must work on mobile and desktop, preserve accessibility, and retain the site's metadata and analytics requirements.

## Brand Commitments

- Publish under the JackMaguire.org identity and in Jack's direct food-guide voice.
- Use Eater's editorial usefulness and decisiveness as a quality reference without copying its branding, language, layout, or trade dress.
- Prefer concrete details, practical tradeoffs, ordinary vocabulary, and short paragraphs.
- Use no em dashes or en dashes.
- Avoid promotional language, generic praise, AI phrasing, and unsupported tasting descriptions.

## Evidence on Hand

- The repository contains published NYC food guides that establish Jack's accepted public voice.
- A preliminary cheese-blintz candidate search exists in the working conversation, but it is not sufficient as the final evidence ledger.
- Current restaurant menus, public review evidence, and frozen-pack publication inventories must be rechecked before publication.
- Jack supplied no personal cheese-blintz experience or first-person anecdote for this guide.

## Product Principles

1. Put the decision before the background.
2. Let evidence determine the ranking and final count.
3. Make every restaurant entry useful at the moment of choosing where to go.
4. Keep editorial confidence proportional to the underlying evidence.
5. Use distinctive design to improve comprehension rather than decorate the article.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The article should meet normal accessible-web expectations: semantic headings, keyboard-safe controls, readable contrast, responsive tables or alternatives, reduced-motion support where relevant, and no information conveyed by color alone.
