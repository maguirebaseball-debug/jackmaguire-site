# Volume and competition analysis for article ideas

Prepared September 20, 2026 for JackMaguire.org.

## Bottom line

The strongest near-term opportunities are not the broadest keywords. “Best ramen in NYC,” “best European cities,” “best AI tools,” and similar head terms have obvious demand, but their results are crowded with major editorial brands, directories, maps, and established specialist sites.

The better opportunities combine a specific decision, a clear place or audience, and evidence Jack can actually provide. The best five from this list are:

1. **How much a 15-day Central Europe trip costs**, built from the completed nine-city trip, tickets, and budget records.
2. **Central Europe itinerary with no one-night stays**, reframed around the real tradeoff between route coverage and travel friction.
3. **Best NYC restaurants worth crossing town for**, using a travel-time and payoff framework rather than another popularity list.
4. **How paid social reporting hides incrementality**, using Jack's paid-social measurement experience and a clear attribution-versus-causality model.
5. **How to search your own notes for article ideas**, using the personal wiki, Search Console, photo metadata, and research-ledger workflow as an original operating guide.

The tsukemen article already published at `/blog/best-tsukemen-nyc/` is a useful test case. It has direct Search Console evidence, but only about 13 combined impressions for the two tracked query variants in the latest complete 30-day window. That is proof of relevance and early indexing, not proof of meaningful market volume.

## How to read the scores

No free source available in this workspace provides reliable monthly keyword volume for all 100 ideas. Search Console reports impressions for Jack's site, not total market searches. Google Trends is relative, and autocomplete reflects related language, not volume. The report therefore uses three separate labels:

- **Demand proxy:** low, medium, or high, based on Search Console rows where available, autocomplete breadth, repeated search intent, and the general size of the topic.
- **Competition:** low, medium, or high, based on the current result types and the strength of ranking domains. This is not a keyword difficulty score.
- **Opportunity:** a 0 to 30 score using demand, growth potential, SERP weakness, Jack advantage, engagement potential, and feasibility. It is a prioritization model, not measured traffic.

The scoring model follows the site's SEO opportunity skill. A score of 23 or more is worth acting on, 18 to 22 needs a source and SERP check, and below 18 is usually a poor use of publishing time.

## What would change the ranking

The biggest missing input is reliable query-level volume. Search Console can show whether Jack's existing pages are beginning to receive impressions, but it cannot prove the total number of searches. Before writing the next page, run the top 15 candidates through the same pipeline:

1. Pull the latest complete 30-day and prior 30-day Search Console query rows, including variants and adjacent pages.
2. Record Google Trends direction for the exact query and two or three close variants in the relevant geography.
3. Collect autocomplete, People also ask, and related-search language. Treat the output as intent evidence, not volume.
4. Inspect the live top 10 results and record whether each is a major editorial guide, local business page, directory, forum, video, map result, or genuinely useful first-person comparison.
5. Check Notion, the Yelp archive, travel tickets, photo metadata, and research ledgers for evidence that can make the page materially better.
6. Use GA4 organic engagement on related pages to decide whether the topic deserves a new page, an update, or only an internal supporting link.

The most useful next measurement artifact would be a table with exact query, Search Console impressions, prior-period impressions, Trends direction, autocomplete variants, top-three result domains, SERP weakness notes, evidence source, and proposed URL. That table would turn this directional triage into a publishable editorial queue.
