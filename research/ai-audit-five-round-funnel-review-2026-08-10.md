# AI Audit five-round funnel review

Date: 2026-08-10

## Starting facts

- The offer has no real purchases yet.
- Jack wants the first 5 to 10 customers so he can learn from actual tasks, reports, objections, and refunds.
- Each report should take about 20 minutes to produce.
- The price stays at $59 during the first cohort.
- Meta continues to optimize for Purchase. BeginCheckout is a diagnostic event, not the optimization event.
- The buyer can be anyone, but the eligible task must be narrow enough to assess honestly.

## Final offer contract

Visible product name: **One-Task Automation Plan**

Primary promise:

> One task you repeat every week. One practical plan to make it faster.

Supporting copy:

> Tell me how you do one recurring digital task. Within one business day after you complete the intake, I will email one recommended AI tool or workflow, three first setup steps, estimated software cost, and projected weekly time saved. If I cannot show a 30-minute weekly opportunity from your answers, I will refund the $59.

Primary CTA:

> Get my plan for $59

Best-fit boundary:

> Best for repeated tasks involving email, notes, spreadsheets, calendars, forms, files, or online research.

Scope boundary:

> You receive the plan, not a built automation. I never need account access, passwords, API keys, or sensitive files.

The report should use one fixed five-block template:

1. The task now, including estimated weekly minutes.
2. One recommended tool or workflow.
3. Three first setup steps.
4. Estimated before and after minutes plus estimated monthly software cost.
5. One assumption, privacy issue, or limitation to test.

The offer does not include implementation, account connections, software fees, custom code, multiple workflows, indefinite revisions, or guaranteed realized savings.

## Round 1: Demand reality and comprehension

The first round found that the page explained more than a typical early offer, but still did not clearly beat the free alternatives.

Key findings:

- The real status quo is manual repetition plus asking ChatGPT for free.
- `Your AI Audit` and `AI Bottleneck Snapshot` sound broader and more technical than the delivered service.
- The sample was weaker than the promise because it did not show a named workflow, clear setup steps, inputs and outputs, cost, or a meaningful limitation.
- The process was scattered across the page rather than shown as one sequence.
- A cold buyer could think the $59 includes implementation.
- The refund wording could be read as a results guarantee even though the actual guarantee concerns a projected opportunity.

Round 1 conclusion: make the human judgment layer, exact deliverable, scope, process, and guarantee literal.

## Round 2: The narrowest honest paid wedge

The second round defined a broad audience with a narrow task contract.

The buyer does not need to be a business owner. The task should be one recurring digital task involving email, notes, spreadsheets, calendars, forms, files, or online research. The page should not imply that every physical, one-off, sensitive, or judgment-heavy task can be automated.

The paid distinction from DIY is:

> You can ask ChatGPT for a list of ideas for free. This purchase is for one human-selected recommendation tied to your actual steps, tools, constraints, time estimate, and budget, with a setup sequence and a refund if I cannot find a credible 30-minute weekly opportunity.

The process should be shown as:

1. Pay $59 through Stripe.
2. Answer eight questions about one task.
3. Jack reviews the steps, tools, time, budget, and constraints.
4. Receive the one-page plan within one business day after the completed intake.
5. If no qualifying opportunity exists, receive a no-fit explanation and full refund instead.

Round 2 conclusion: sell one decision and plan for one task, not a broad AI audit.

## Round 3: Guarantee, privacy, and reputation risk

The third round separated the objective guarantee from achieved results.

Recommended guarantee copy:

> I use the task time, steps, tools, and constraints you provide to estimate the current and recommended workflow. If I cannot identify one practical change that projects at least 30 minutes saved per week, I will email you a short explanation and initiate a full $59 refund to your original payment method. You do not have to implement anything to receive this no-fit refund.

> This is an opportunity guarantee, not a results guarantee. If the report identifies a qualifying opportunity, actual savings still depend on your setup, usage, and the accuracy of your estimates.

Use `I will initiate a full refund`, not `automatic refund`, unless the refund is actually created without Jack's action. Refunds return to the original payment method. Stripe says the credit usually appears in approximately 5 to 10 business days, depending on the bank.

Recommended privacy copy:

> Describe the task, but do not send passwords, API keys, login links, full customer lists, medical or financial records, confidential source files, or other sensitive data. I will not log into or connect your accounts. Redacted examples and rough estimates are enough.

Do not add a broad satisfaction guarantee before observing real objections. If a delivered report omits one of the five promised blocks, Jack can correct it once at no charge when notified within 7 days. This is a delivery correction, not open-ended support.

Round 3 conclusion: one objective no-fit guarantee builds more trust than overlapping, subjective refund promises.

## Round 4: One funnel and learning system

The fourth round treated the ads, landing page, checkout, intake, report, and refund as one system.

The three ads should vary the repeated task family:

1. Repeated emails and follow-ups.
2. Repeated spreadsheet and reporting work.
3. Repeated planning and personal admin.

Everything else should remain stable during the first cohort:

- $59 price.
- Purchase optimization.
- Shared offer sentence.
- Product name and CTA.
- Report contract.
- Refund terms.
- Audience and ad set.
- Landing page.
- Fulfillment template.
- Attribution fields and event definitions.

The founder learning record can be one spreadsheet row per purchase with these fields:

- Ad concept and landing session.
- Task family and buyer wording.
- Current minutes per week.
- Recommended tool or workflow.
- Projected minutes saved and estimated software cost.
- Intake completion time.
- Report delivery time.
- No-fit reason and refund status.
- Whether the buyer tried the recommendation.

Round 4 conclusion: keep the buyer identity broad, vary only the task-family hook, and evaluate eligible paid orders rather than clicks alone.

## Round 5: Adversarial launch review

The fifth round removed work that would delay proof or expand a 20-minute service.

The main risk is not insufficient analytics. It is promising a broad AI audit while delivering a short recommendation. The smallest coherent launch includes:

- Plain product and CTA language.
- The full offer contract in or immediately below the hero.
- A short three-step process.
- One included and excluded block.
- One faithful five-block sample.
- The opportunity guarantee next to the refund claim.
- Relevant, verifiable founder credibility.
- A working attribution chain from ad through Purchase and intake.

The first 5 to 10 buyers are product discovery. They are not enough to establish a statistically reliable creative winner or stable Meta CPA.

## Initial paid risk cap

Do not spend $10,000 while the offer has zero purchase proof.

The initial paid test needs a hard loss cap. Use the lesser of:

- $590, equal to ten sale prices.
- An amount Jack can comfortably lose without affecting essential expenses.

Because Jack is unemployed, the practical cap should likely be lower than $590. This is a risk ceiling, not a spending target. Organic or direct first-customer outreach can run alongside the paid test and must use a distinct source value.

## Launch decision rules

### Before spend

1. Test the mobile path from each ad URL through the landing page, Stripe, intake, and success state.
2. Confirm each ad uses its stable `utm_content` value.
3. Confirm one successful payment produces one deduplicated Meta Purchase with value 59 and currency USD.
4. Confirm the five-block report can be completed from a realistic intake in about 20 minutes.

### During the first traffic

1. Freeze the $59 price and core contract until five eligible reports are delivered unless a factual error must be corrected.
2. If one ad receives 50 landing page views and zero checkout starts, revise that task hook or its match with the page. Do not change price.
3. If the funnel produces five checkout starts and zero purchases, inspect checkout function, trust, and offer clarity before buying more traffic.
4. If the campaign reaches 100 landing page views with zero purchases, pause paid traffic and revise the offer or page.
5. Stop at the initial risk cap even if the view thresholds have not been reached.

### After the first 5 paid orders

1. If two or more become no-fit refunds, tighten eligible-task copy before reopening or increasing spend.
2. If fewer than four buyers complete intake, fix the checkout-to-intake handoff.
3. If the same expectation mismatch appears twice, correct the page and report contract.
4. If at least four reports are delivered on time, continue toward ten without repricing.
5. Ask each buyer: `What made you buy now?`, `What did you expect to receive?`, and `Was the next step specific enough to try?`

### After the first 10 eligible delivered orders

1. Choose a narrower task-family wedge only if one family accounts for at least half of eligible orders or clearly leads on purchase rate, low no-fit rate, and setup attempts.
2. If no family dominates, keep the broad recurring digital-task offer and collect ten more orders.
3. Scale paid spend only if observed cost per eligible paid order and net revenue are acceptable.
4. If acquisition cost remains above net revenue and there is no repeat purchase or additional offer, treat the campaign as paid validation rather than a scalable acquisition channel.
5. Only after this review should Jack test $69, a second deliverable, or a bounded implementation offer.

## Ship now

- Plain one-task product and CTA language.
- One recurring digital-task boundary with concrete examples.
- One five-block deliverable.
- One three-step process.
- One opportunity guarantee and no-fit refund process.
- One faithful sample that matches actual fulfillment.
- Three task-family ads in both required formats.
- Stable ad UTMs, Purchase tracking, intake attribution, and a simple founder learning sheet.

## Defer

- Ad-specific landing pages.
- Dynamic headline personalization.
- A second satisfaction guarantee.
- Price tests.
- Multiple service tiers and upsells.
- Automated report generation or no-fit decisions.
- A custom cohort database or complex dashboard.
- Statistical creative winner claims from the first cohort.
- Indefinite support, revisions, implementation, or account access.
