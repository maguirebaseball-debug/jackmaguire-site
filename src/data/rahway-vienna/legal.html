Vienna (Wien) and Austrian law

A few current facts about how Vienna is governed, how Austria's courts work, and rules that matter when you stay.

At a glance

Official sources are linked with each fact.

01

Vienna is both a city and a province

Vienna, called Wien in German, is Austria's capital and largest municipality. It is also one of the country's nine federal provinces. Its 100-member City Council also serves as the Provincial Parliament, so the same elected body handles municipal and provincial roles.

02

Austrian citizens can vote at 16

Austria lowered the voting age to 16 in 2007. Austrian citizens who are at least 16 on election day can vote in national elections. Non-Austrian EU citizens resident in Austria can vote in European Parliament elections and local council elections, which in Vienna means district council elections, subject to registration rules.

03

The Constitutional Court has a distinct job

Austria's Constitutional Court reviews whether laws and regulations comply with the Constitution. It can annul unconstitutional provisions and also decides certain election disputes and conflicts over which public authority has jurisdiction.

04

Taking up a residence comes with a registration deadline

If you move into an apartment, house or room as a residence, you generally must register with the local authority within three days. The official guidance says a person staying in a dwelling for no more than three days is exempt from that registration requirement.

Residence registration guidance

05

Permanent neutrality is written into constitutional law

On October 26, 1955, Austria's National Council adopted the constitutional law on permanent neutrality. October 26 is now Austria's National Day.
