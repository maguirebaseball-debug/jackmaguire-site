If you find yourself on Foster Avenue in Ditmas Park with a friend and a table at **The Ox Tavern** (1715 Foster Ave, sharing space with Westwood Bar), you are looking at a menu with two distinct operational engines: the legacy of the neighborhood's legendary *Ox Cart Tavern* butcher-blend burgers, and Chef Julio Salazar’s scratch Mexican and global bistro plates.

Most diners treat it as either a generic neighborhood burger bar or a standard craft beer pub. Both approaches leave serious flavor on the table.

If you don't care about forced "category diversity" and strictly want the **purest expression of peak deliciousness, textural contrast, and flavor velocity**, here is the exact order for two people, the food science behind why it works, and the operational protocol to make sure the kitchen executes it flawlessly.

## 1. The Burger: Why The "Good Ol' Boy" Dominates

The Ox Tavern offers a respectable Burger Board - the classic Ox, the avocado-topped Cali-Patty, the chili-spiked La Vaca, and the popular Alamo (topped with an onion ring, runny egg, and BBQ sauce).

While the Alamo delivers fun textural crunch, the **Good Ol' Boy** is the kitchen’s pure flavor peak:

* **The Triple Nucleotide-Glutamate Multiplier:** The burger patty is an 80/20 coarse butcher grind of chuck, brisket, and short rib. When you combine seared beef (glutamic acid) with sautéed cremini mushrooms (guanylic acid) and thick-cut smoked bacon (inosinic acid), you trigger a textbook nucleotide synergy. Food science demonstrates that pairing these three compounds multiplies perceived savoriness by up to $8\times$ compared to beef alone.
* **The Cheese Sheath:** Sharp aged cheddar melted under a basting dome directly over the patty creates an elastic dairy seal, locking the sautéed mushroom caps to the meat crown and preventing topping fallout.
* **The Medium-Rare Requirement (132°F):** Do not order this Medium. An 80/20 butcher grind renders its intramuscular fat perfectly at medium-rare while preserving intracellular moisture. Pushing past 140°F expels cellular water and leaves the patty dense.
* **The Butter-Toasted Brioche Heel:** The kitchen toasts the brioche on the flat-top with butter, forming a hydrophobic crust that repels mushroom juices and prevents bottom-bun sogginess.

> **The Execution Call:** Ask your server to have the kitchen **cut the burger in half with sandwich skewers**. Halving it hot in the kitchen with a commercial serrated blade seals the bun crumb cleanly; trying to saw through thick bacon and mushrooms at a small table with a dull butter knife just crushes the burger.

## 2. The Heavyweight: Quesabirria Tacos & Consommé Immersion

Skipping the seafood entrees (like the salmon or fish & chips) frees you up to order what the kitchen does with supreme scratch confidence: **Quesabirria Tacos**.

* **The Shell:** Corn tortillas dipped in spiced birria fat and griddled hard on the flat-top, yielding a brittle, crunchy exterior with melted, elastic Oaxaca cheese inside.
* **The Braise:** Hours of slow-simmering beef chuck with guajillo, ancho, and Mexican oregano break down tough connective tissue into succulent, gelatin-coated shreds.
* **The Double-Dip Technique:** Submerge each taco bite into the piping-hot bone broth consommé, take a bite, and then dip the newly exposed shredded beef edge back into the broth for second-bite juicy saturation.

## 3. The Starch Alpha: The Dual-Dip French Fry

The Good Ol' Boy comes with house-cut fries. Request them **extra crispy**. This extra minute in the fryer drives out core water, giving the potato crust a 20-minute crispness lifespan rather than going limp after eight minutes.

Here is the move: **Dip your fries directly into the hot Quesabirria consommé.** 

The salted potato starch drinks in the spiced beef-tallow broth like a French dip sandwich. Follow that with a dip into a side ramekin of **fresh scratch chimichurri** (the parsley-garlic-vinegar sauce made for the skirt steak - ask for a side ramekin nicely). The sharp red wine vinegar and raw parsley immediately slice through the beef fat.

> **Pro-Tip:** Stir a spoonful of chimichurri directly into the remaining birria broth halfway through. The raw garlic and red wine vinegar brighten the rich beef tallow instantly.

## 4. The Palate Primer: Crispy Togarashi Brussels Sprouts

A meal with this much mammalian fat requires an aggressive bitter-and-acid counterweight.

The **Crispy Brussels Sprouts** are flash-fried until the exterior leaves blister into paper-thin, charred crisps. The kitchen tosses them in *Shichimi Togarashi* (Japanese 7-spice: red chili, sansho pepper, dried orange peel, nori, and sesame seeds).

* **The Rule:** Ask for the chipotle mayo **on the side**. Drizzling warm mayo over fried sprouts wilts the delicate charred leaves within four minutes. Keeping it on the side lets you dip as needed while preserving maximum crunch.
* **The Acid Activation:** Squeeze the fresh lime wedge across the entire bowl immediately upon arrival. Citric acid cuts through the natural glucosinolate bitterness and creates a bright glaze over the togarashi seasoning.

## 5. Beverage Mechanics: How to Strip Tongue-Coating Tallow

Between sharp cheddar, smoked bacon, 80/20 ground beef, braised birria, and Oaxaca cheese, your palate will experience fat fatigue if you drink water alone.

* **Craft IPA (The Bitter Stripper):** The alpha-acid humulones in a local dry/hoppy IPA physically break the surface tension of melted fat on your tongue, clearing your taste buds for the next bite.
* **Mezcalita (The Smoke & Acid Echo):** The agave smoke notes match the woodsmoke of the bacon and birria, while the lime juice keeps the finish crisp.
* **Non-Alcoholic Pick:** Club soda with fresh lime juice. The carbonic acid bubbles lift oil off the palate cleanly without adding sugar.

## The 4 Costly Ordering Traps to Avoid

1. **The Double-Burger Trap:** Ordering two burgers duplicates bread carbs and patty textures, driving flavor fatigue up by 80%. One burger + one birria taco plate delivers handheld burger craft plus immersion broth.
2. **The Westwood Nachos Trap:** The nachos are huge (~1,200 kcal) and heavy with beans and sour cream. They completely destroy your appetite for the burger and tacos.
3. **The Simultaneous Drop Trap:** If the kitchen drops appetizers and mains together, the burger cools below 115°F (cheese solidifies, bun steams) while you eat sprouts. Always stage courses.
4. **The Overcooked Patty Trap:** Never order above Medium-Rare. An 80/20 custom butcher blend loses up to 22% of its internal moisture when pushed to Medium or Well-Done.

## Tactical Pacing & Exact Server Script

Neighborhood gastropub kitchens can get slammed between 7:00 PM and 8:30 PM. To guarantee peak temperature and cooking focus:

* **Target Timing:** Arrive between **5:15 PM and 6:00 PM**. You avoid the line rush, get the line cook's full attention on your burger sear, and capture **Happy Hour** ($6 draft beers, $8 cocktails).
* **Transit Geometry:** Take the **Q Train to Newkirk Plaza**. Walk 3 minutes north on East 17th Street to Foster Avenue.
* **Table Location:** Ask for a center booth. It shields your table from cold drafts coming through the front door, keeping your consommé and burger cheese hot.
* **Pacing:** Tell the server you'd like the **Brussels Sprouts fired first**, and you'll put your mains in when the sprouts arrive.
