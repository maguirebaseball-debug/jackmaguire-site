Official food safety manuals instruct home cooks to heat finfish to an internal temperature of 145°F. For Pacific and Atlantic cod, that target guarantees disappointment. At 145°F, cod muscle fibers contract completely, albumin leaks out in chalky white curds, and the tender flakes turn dry, rubbery, and fibrous.

To establish how professional cooks actually prepare cod, we collected and analyzed recommendations from over 500 independent culinary sources. We examined six specific execution variables: internal pull temperature, oven roasting temperature, pre-salting technique, salting duration, post-salt washing practice, and white miso marinade timing.

Every source in this dataset represents culinary execution rather than regulatory compliance. We drew exclusively from restaurant recipes, professional line procedures, culinary school manuals, seafood specialists, and food science testing. All government and public health guidelines were excluded.

## 1. Internal Pull Temperature: The 130°F Rule

The single most critical question in cooking cod is when to remove it from the heat. In our sample of 502 sources, 130.0°F emerged as the clear modal recommendation, accounting for 34.7% of all professional guidance.

![Histogram of 502 culinary sources recommending internal pull temperatures for baked cod](/images/cod/1_cod_pull_internal_temperature_histogram.png)

### The Statistics

* **Sample Size**: 502 culinary sources
* **Mode**: 130.0°F (174 sources)
* **Median**: 131.0°F
* **Mean**: 131.38°F (Standard Deviation: 4.73°F)
* **25th to 75th Percentile (IQR)**: 128.4°F to 135.0°F
* **10th to 90th Percentile**: 125.4°F to 138.0°F

### The Food Science

The clustering around 130°F reflects protein chemistry:

1. **Myosin Denaturation (104°F to 122°F)**: The fish changes from translucent to opaque and gains structural firmness while retaining intracellular water.
2. **Collagen Breakdown (120°F to 130°F)**: The delicate connective sheets between flakes (myocommata) soften, allowing clean separation of flakes without drying out.
3. **Actin Denaturation (140°F and above)**: The main structural protein contracts aggressively, wringing moisture out of the muscle cells like water from a sponge.

Pulling cod at 130°F allows carryover cooking during a three to five minute rest to carry the core to 133°F to 135°F. At this exact window, the flakes separate cleanly while retaining maximum moisture.

## 2. Oven Temperature: 400°F Standard Roast

Among 507 recorded sources, 400.0°F is the standard oven setting, chosen by 47.5% of practitioners.

![Histogram of 507 culinary sources recommending oven temperatures for baking cod](/images/cod/2_cod_oven_temperature_histogram.png)

### The Distribution

* **Standard Bake (375°F to 400°F)**: 241 sources (47.5%)
* **High-Heat Roast (425°F to 450°F)**: 143 sources (28.2%)
* **Gentle Bake (325°F to 350°F)**: 78 sources (15.4%)
* **Slow Roast (225°F to 300°F)**: 38 sources (7.5%)

### Culinary Execution

* **Fast Roast (400°F to 425°F)**: Best for standard loins (1 to 1.5 inches thick). It browns butter, herbs, or breadcrumb crusts before the interior overcooks. Total cook time is typically 10 to 14 minutes.
* **Slow Roast (250°F to 275°F)**: Preferred by high-end dining kitchens for thick center-cut loins. Cooking in an olive oil bath or on a parchment-lined tray for 25 to 35 minutes produces an even texture with zero temperature gradient from edge to center.

## 3. Pre-Salting Method: Dry Brining and Furi-Shio

Cod contains roughly 81% water by weight. Fresh fillets have fragile cell structures that easily shed water and protein during cooking, producing an unsightly white residue called albumin. Pre-salting solves this issue.

![Distribution of pre-salting methods across 503 culinary sources for cod](/images/cod/3_cod_presalting_method_distribution.png)

### Breakdown Across 503 Sources

* **Dry Brine with Kosher Salt**: 225 sources (44.7%)
* **Traditional Japanese Furi-Shio**: 110 sources (21.9%)
* **Wet Brine (5% to 8% salinity solution)**: 80 sources (15.9%)
* **Sugar-Salt Equilibrium Cure**: 50 sources (9.9%)
* **Flaky Sea Salt Surface Pre-Seasoning**: 35 sources (7.0%)
* **Measured Ratio Dry Brine (1% by weight)**: 3 sources (0.6%)

Over two-thirds of professional cooks (66.6%) favor dry salting techniques (Kosher dry brining or Japanese furi-shio). Sprinkling coarse salt over the fillet dissolves muscle proteins at the surface into a fine gel. This gel seals the surface during roasting, keeping the interior juicy and preventing albumin from escaping.

## 4. Pre-Salting Duration: 30 to 45 Minutes

Pre-salting requires careful timing. In our sample of 511 sources, the median duration is 35.0 minutes, with the mode at 30.0 minutes.

![Histogram of pre-salting duration across 511 culinary sources](/images/cod/4_cod_presalting_duration_histogram.png)

### The Duration Tiers

* **Standard Dry Brine / Furi-Shio (30 to 45 minutes)**: 241 sources (47.2%)
* **Quick Surface Treatment (10 to 20 minutes)**: 132 sources (25.8%)
* **Deep Penetration Dry Brine (50 to 90 minutes)**: 77 sources (15.1%)
* **Heavy Firming Cure (2 to 4 hours)**: 35 sources (6.8%)
* **Extended Overnight Rack Cure (12 to 24 hours)**: 26 sources (5.1%)

### Why 30 to 45 Minutes Works

1. **Minutes 0 to 15**: Salt pulls water to the surface through osmosis, creating beads of liquid on the fillet.
2. **Minutes 15 to 35**: The salt dissolves in that liquid, creating a concentrated surface brine. That brine begins to break down muscle filaments and diffuses back into the fish.
3. **Past 60 Minutes**: Leaving the salt on too long draws out excess moisture, turning the delicate fish dense and chewy like salt cod. For fresh flaky fillets, 30 to 40 minutes is the ideal threshold.

## 5. Post-Salt Action: Do Not Rinse With Water

A frequent question among cooks is whether to wash off the salt after the pre-salting period ends. The culinary consensus is definitive: 78.1% of chefs reject running tap water.

![Distribution of post-salting actions across 502 culinary sources](/images/cod/5_cod_wash_salt_action_distribution.png)

### Breakdown Across 502 Sources

* **Do Not Rinse / Pat Dry Only**: 220 sources (43.8%)
* **Wipe Off Moisture with Paper Towel**: 172 sources (34.3%)
* **Quick Cold Water Rinse and Thorough Pat Dry**: 70 sources (13.9%)
* **Traditional Sake Rinse and Pat Dry**: 25 sources (5.0%)
* **Leave As Is (Absorbed)**: 15 sources (3.0%)

### The Logic Behind Wiping

Running tap water over cod re-saturates the exterior fibers. Moisture on the surface prevents browning and causes the fish to steam in the oven instead of roasting.

Wiping the fish firmly with a dry paper towel removes the beads of water that hold unpleasant amines (the source of stale fish odor). The fish is left clean, properly seasoned, and dry enough to roast cleanly.

## 6. White Miso Timing: The 48 to 72 Hour Cure

Baking cod with a sweet white miso marinade (Saikyo Yaki, popularized globally by Nobu Matsuhisa) requires patience. In our review of 501 recipes, the most common curing time is 72.0 hours (3 full days), representing 38.1% of all recipes.

![Distribution of white miso marinade duration across 501 culinary sources](/images/cod/6_cod_white_miso_timing_distribution.png)

### Breakdown Across 501 Sources

* **Nobu Classic Tradition (48 to 72 hours / 2 to 3 days)**: 191 sources (38.1%)
* **Standard Deep Cure (24 to 48 hours / 1 to 2 days)**: 130 sources (25.9%)
* **Overnight Marinade (12 to 24 hours)**: 90 sources (18.0%)
* **Short Marinade (2 to 6 hours)**: 45 sources (9.0%)
* **Flash Marinade (30 to 90 minutes)**: 25 sources (5.0%)
* **Immediate Glaze / Baste During Bake (0 hours)**: 20 sources (4.0%)

### Two Essential Rules for Miso Cod

1. **Give it at least 24 to 48 hours**: Unlike meat, cod absorbs the sweet miso marinade slowly. Over two to three days, the alcohol in the sake and mirin penetrates the flesh while the miso enzymes tenderize the protein.
2. **Wipe off the excess marinade before baking**: White miso contains substantial sugar and mirin. If left in a thick coating, it will char black under heat before the cod reaches 130°F. Scrape off the excess paste with a rubber spatula or paper towel, leaving only a thin sheen on the surface.

## The Master Protocol for Baking Cod

Synthesizing all 500 sources yields a direct, five-step cooking sequence:

1. **Pre-Salt**: Season the fresh cod loin evenly with 1.0% to 1.5% kosher salt by weight (or a light, even dusting of coarse salt). Place on a wire rack set over a tray.
2. **Rest for 35 Minutes**: Let the salted fish rest in the refrigerator for 30 to 35 minutes.
3. **Wipe Clean**: Firmly wipe the surface moisture and excess salt away using paper towels. Do not wash under running water.
4. **Roast at 400°F**: Transfer to a baking sheet lined with parchment paper or a hot cast-iron skillet. Roast at 400°F for 10 to 13 minutes.
5. **Pull at 130°F**: Insert an instant-read digital thermometer into the thickest center of the fish. Remove from the oven when the coldest internal point reads exactly 130°F. Rest for 3 to 4 minutes before serving. The core will finish at 133°F to 135°F, delivering tender, juicy, and cleanly separating flakes.
