Temple Bar is an expensive caricature of Irish pub culture. 

Between nine-euro pints, stag parties, and cover bands playing the same pop covers on loop, the neighborhood feels built entirely for tourists who have never left the city center.

If you want to understand why Dublin pub culture actually matters, skip the riverfront tourist strip and visit two historic institutions: Mulligans on Poolbeg Street and John Kavanagh, known as The Gravediggers, in Glasnevin.

## Comparing Dublin Heritage Pubs

Here is how authentic heritage bars compare with typical tourist pubs in central Dublin.

| Pub | Neighborhood | Pint Price (Guinness) | Atmosphere & Rules | Typical Crowd |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| John Kavanagh (The Gravediggers) | Glasnevin (Northside) | 5.80 to 6.20 EUR | No TVs, no music, Victorian timber, quiet conversation | Locals, cemetery visitors, pub purists |
| Mulligans | Poolbeg St (City Centre East) | 5.90 to 6.30 EUR | Historic snugs, wooden partitions, fast pints | Office workers, journalists, regulars |

## Mulligans on Poolbeg Street

Mulligans has stood on Poolbeg Street since 1782. 

It sits just two blocks south of the River Liffey, tucked behind Tara Street Station and the George's Quay office complexes. Despite being five minutes from the O'Connell Bridge, few tourists find it.

Step through the front door into the main bar:
* The walls are dark with age, and the worn wooden counters have seen generations of newspaper reporters from the nearby Irish Press and dockworkers from the river.
* There is no background music playlist competing with your conversation. The dominant sound is the hum of people talking and the thud of heavy pint glasses settling onto coasters.
* The Guinness pour here is consistently ranked among the top three in the city: cold without being iced, dense foam dome, and clean bitter finish.

Order your pint at the bar, pay with contactless card or cash, and grab an empty wooden stool in the back room.

## How to Visit The Gravediggers in Glasnevin

John Kavanagh sits at 1 Prospect Square in Glasnevin on Dublin's Northside. 

Opened in 1833, the pub shares an old stone party wall with Glasnevin Cemetery. Gravediggers would historically knock on the pub's exterior wall to have pints passed through a hatch after digging graves, earning the bar its lasting nickname.

Getting here is simple:
* From central Dublin (College Green or Westmoreland Street), board Dublin Bus Route 83 heading north toward Harristown.
* Ride roughly fifteen minutes to the Botanic Road stop.
* Walk three minutes east through the quiet residential brick terrace into Prospect Square. The pub sits at the corner with an unpainted wooden exterior and simple gold lettering.

### Etiquette Inside the Front Snug

The interior is divided into two distinct zones: the front traditional bar and the back lounge.

The front bar is where you want to sit:
* The woodwork has never been stripped or modernised. The worn pine floorboards, gas lamp fittings, and aged partitions feel preserved from the nineteenth century.
* Pub etiquette is taken seriously here: speak at a conversational volume, keep your phone tucked away, and do not expect televisions showing sports matches.
* Order a pint of plain (Guinness). The tap lines are short, the cellar temperature is calibrated, and the pour is legendary across Ireland.

Spending three quiet hours in the corner snug of The Gravediggers on a weekday afternoon is the best cultural experience in Dublin.

![A perfectly poured pint of Guinness resting on the weathered wooden bar at The Gravediggers](/images/europe-2026/dublin-gravediggers-pint.jpg)

Take Bus 83 out to Glasnevin around four in the afternoon, get your pint at the front bar of Kavanagh's, and leave Temple Bar to the stag parties.
